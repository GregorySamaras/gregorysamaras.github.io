The Security Fear Economy

Fear is one of the most effective currencies in many industries, and security is no exception.

Every major cyberattack seems to create another sales opportunity. A company suffers a breach, and shortly afterwards someone is explaining how their security platform could have prevented it.

At conferences, the demonstrations are impressive, products are increasingly AI-powered, and the possibility of becoming the next headline is difficult for a CISO to ignore. The concern is legitimate; nobody wants to dismiss something that could help prevent an incident.

What concerns me is how easily that pressure can turn into a purchase before anyone establishes what is actually missing. Security leaders don't need to run every platform themselves, but the people who do should be part of the discussion. They know which tools are already available, which capabilities haven't been configured and where the real operational gaps are.

Every additional platform needs integration, maintenance, tuning and someone to investigate its findings. Without the time and people to do that work, it becomes another dashboard, another subscription and potentially another source of alerts that distract from the ones that matter.

Sometimes improving security might mean buying less and understanding more. Before purchasing something new, I'd rather see an honest assessment of what the organisation already owns, what it uses properly and whether another product would solve a problem the existing tools cannot.
